8-Week Hip Mobility & Pelvic Floor Guide

Hips tipping forward, tight groin, weak pelvic floor — one short session a day.

~22–39 min/day, plus cardio. Complete each week in order.

D1D2D3D4D5D6D7

Each week builds on the last — finish all 7 days, then move to the next week.

The 3 rules for hips that tilt forward

  • Stretch the front: hip flexors are usually the tightest link — that's the half-kneeling stretch.
  • Strengthen the back: glutes + deep core pull the pelvis level — bridges, kickbacks, dead bugs, planks.
  • Move from neutral: in every exercise, tuck the tailbone slightly and keep ribs down — no overarching the lower back.

Daily pelvic floor block (Mojo-style)

Every day includes the full set: pulses for strength, holds for endurance, and reverse kegels for release. The doses progress with W1–W8, while the day's hip openers and deep breathing cover relaxation.

Day 1 — Reset & Realign

Learn neutral pelvis; gentle full-body reset. ~22–30 min before cardio, depending on week.

Diaphragm Breathing

Mojo

2 min

Inhale: belly rises, pelvic floor drops. Exhale: let it all relax. On every inhale, feel your pelvic floor drop and relax.

Why: Calms tight pelvic floor and hip flexors before strengthening.

Cat-Cow

Mobility

8 slow reps

Move with your breath; don't force the end ranges.

Why: Teaches your pelvis to tilt both ways so you can find neutral.

Glute Bridge

Strength

2–3 × 10–12, 2-sec hold

Exhale, squeeze glutes, tuck tailbone slightly at the top.

Why: Key move: strong glutes pull the pelvis out of a forward tilt.

Dead Bug

Core

2 × 6–8 / side

Press your lower back into the floor; exhale as you extend.

Why: Trains deep core to hold the pelvis neutral instead of tipping forward.

90/90 Hip Switches

Mobility

2 × 5–6 / side

Slow and controlled; keep your chest tall.

Why: Opens internal and external hip rotation evenly.

Frog Stretch

Groin

2 × 45–60 sec

Knees wide, rock gently; mild stretch only, never sharp.

Why: Releases inner-thigh and groin tightness that tugs the pelvis forward.

Day 2 — Build the Glutes

Strength day: glutes do the correcting. ~22–28 min before cardio, depending on week.

Glute Bridge

Strength

2–3 × 10–12, 2-sec hold

Exhale, squeeze glutes, tuck tailbone slightly at the top.

Why: Key move: strong glutes pull the pelvis out of a forward tilt.

Glute Kickback

Strength

2 × 10 / side

Squeeze the glute to lift; don't arch your lower back.

Why: Builds glute strength without extending the spine.

Reverse Lunge

Strength

2 × 8 / side

Torso tall; tuck the pelvis under slightly at the bottom.

Why: Strengthens glutes and quads while stretching the back-leg hip flexor.

Bird Dog

Core

2 × 6–8 / side

Hips level, reach long; brace your core lightly.

Why: Glute + core coordination for a stable pelvis.

Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch

Stretch

2 × 40 sec / side

Tuck tailbone, ribs down; gently squeeze the back-side glute.

Why: The priority stretch: tight hip flexors are the main driver of forward pelvic tilt.

Butterfly

Groin

2 × 45 sec

Sit tall; ease the knees down, no bouncing.

Why: Opens the groin while you practice sitting tall instead of slouching.

floor

Kegel Holds — endurance (Mojo)

Mojo

5-sec squeeze, 5-sec relax × 8–10, 2 sets

Build up to 10-sec holds over weeks.

Zip-Up Engagement

Core

8 reps × 2 sets, 5-sec holds

Standing tall, imagine zipping up from your pubic bone to your ribs — draw your belly button up and in. This sets your pelvis so your hip doesn't drop as you move. Breathe normally.

Why: Deep-core engagement that stabilizes the pelvis so the hip stays level as you move.
